# Style references from the owner

What the owner wants the game to look like, as pictures and short clips. Every agent working on
the look reads this file first. Use the references for direction (colour, light, brushwork, the
figure, the level of detail), never to copy one specific game: we make our own worlds.

The repository is public. Keep an image here only if the owner made it or it is free to share;
for anything else, keep only the written note (what to take from it) and ask the owner to
upload it again to the session that needs it.

Sorted by the coordinator at the owner's request; the owner has the last word on every line.

**What came.** The clip of reference 1 itself (25 s), seven frames from it, a list of 35 linked
pictures (paintings and other games) and eleven links on painterly rendering.

**What was seen by eye.** The clip (a frame every half second, and four sets of consecutive
frames to judge motion), the seven frames, and one linked picture (Rime). The other 34 pictures
and the rendering pages could not be opened from the cloud session (its network settings refuse
those sites). They are sorted from knowledge of the works, not from the exact picture.

**The measure (the owner's words, docs/HANDOVER.md section 2, items 12 and 13).** The
moving-painting look first; a game that also looks better and runs smoother; a style that is the
game's own.

### What the clip does (seen frame by frame)

1. **The strokes are on the world, not on the screen.** The ground, the trunks, the snow, the
   water and the cloak each carry their own strokes. In consecutive frames the same strokes are
   there again, moved with the ground, and they get smaller with distance. So they do not swim
   or boil when the camera moves. This is not a filter over the finished picture.
2. **Each stroke is one flat colour with a crisp edge,** a little lighter or darker than its
   neighbours, and it runs the way the surface runs: up a trunk, along a plank, across a slope.
3. **Light.** One low, strong sun. Long cast shadows across the ground. Haze that fades the far
   trunks to pale gold, so the wood has depth. Light shafts through the haze.
4. **Colour.** Warm light against cool violet shadow. One accent in the opposite colour: the
   blue cloak in the gold wood, the orange pool of lantern light on the blue snow at night. The
   night is blue and green and readable, never black.
5. **Shapes made of strokes.** Leaves, grass blades, fir boughs and the cloak are built from
   stroke-shaped pieces, so their outlines are ragged like paint.
6. **Things that move.** Falling leaves, the cloak, the aurora.

**Where the game is today** (docs/shots/art, docs/shots/painter/r1): pale washes on paper, even
light, little cast shadow, low contrast. The distance to the clip is mostly light and colour,
then the surface; a filter alone would not cross it.

### The recipe: the clip's look, made the game's own

**The base, from the clip.** Strokes on the world's surfaces; a low strong sun with cast shadows
and haze; warm light against cool shadow; one opposite accent; a blue, readable night.

**What the other references add** (sources in brackets). A proposal, to be set against the code
in the one-page plan; the first six are meant for the prototype.

1. **Paint that re-forms from a leaf to orbit.** Stroke size follows height in steps and
   importance: fine where one must read, broad elsewhere, every stroke crisp (11-11, Tom Thomson,
   Skyward Sword done crisply, Breath of the Wild, The Pathless). The clip never leaves the
   ground; this is the game's own.
2. **A ground colour for each planet showing between the strokes** (Tom Thomson), with three
   colours that key the planet (No Man's Sky, Journey, Firewatch).
3. **A stroke habit for each thing** (Van Gogh): dashes on the ground, strokes up trunks, upright
   strokes in the aurora, and sky strokes that follow the game's real wind. The wind already
   exists in the physics; no reference has that.
4. **Night:** blue with the snow lightest (Sohlberg), snow lit by the aurora (The Long Dark),
   the lantern's pool as a mosaic of warm strokes, mirrored in ice and water (Afremov).
5. **The figure:** outline first (Journey), a rim of light so it always reads (Ori), the cape's
   motion (Sky), the lantern as the one warm accent.
6. **Low-sun glow:** leaves and grass lit from behind (South of Midnight, the clip), warm light
   bounced into shadows (Tiny Glade), shadows with colour.
7. Depth as flat steps of colour (Firewatch): cheap, so it suits phones.
8. Land told apart from the air by colour and lightness: paths, water, trees (Breath of the
   Wild); a landmark in each region (The Pathless, Rime, Clair Obscur).
9. Water: light through wave crests, a glitter path (Sea of Thieves), foam lines (Rime).
10. Living air: leaves, snow, motes (the clip, Ori, Arise); wind shown as streaks in flight
    (Aloft, The Pathless).
11. Glints on snow and sand (Journey); footprints in snow (the clip).
12. Brushed outlines (the rendering links, the clip); hand-made unevenness (Tiny Glade).
13. Views composed for the sketchbook (Eastshade).
14. Planets and rings painted boldly in the sky; the air's colour changing on the way down
    (No Man's Sky, Europa).

### The coordinator's ideas from the sort (put to the owner; not agreed yet)

- **Method.** Paint the strokes on the surfaces (in each material's shading, and in the shapes
  of leaves, grass and cloak), not as a filter over the finished picture. This is what the clip
  does, it stays still in motion, and every stroke keeps a crisp edge. The earlier brief
  (docs/HANDOVER.md section 4a: an edge-following filter in `src/render/post.js`) would not give
  this look. A light finishing pass may still help; it is not the look.
- **Light and colour before strokes.** A low strong sun, cast shadows, haze for depth, warm
  against cool, one opposite accent. Most of the distance to the clip is here.
- **What could be the game's own.** The clip never leaves the ground. This game goes from a leaf
  to orbit. Strokes that re-form as one rises (leaves, then trees, then woods and fields, then
  the planet as a painted ball) would be a painting one can fly through, and from the ship each
  stroke would be a field or a clump of trees with a crisp edge, which serves "see where I'm
  landing". Stroke size has to follow distance in steps so it never shrinks to noise or grows to
  blobs.
- **The cloak's colour (decided by the owner: blue-grey).** In the clip the cloak is the
  opposite colour of the scene, which is why the figure stands out. Plain grey would stand out
  less. So: a blue-grey that takes each world's light, with the lantern as the warm accent.
- **Also decided by the owner:** the whole clip is the target, with no favourite part; no cabins
  or shelters for now; the hood goes up in snow and storms.
- **Risks.** A bigger job than a filter (every world's surfaces get new shading). The clip shows
  three small hand-built scenes; this game has ten planets built by rule. Prove it on one or two
  scenes first (Earth's autumn woodland and Neptune's aurora tundra already exist). The game's
  rendering code has not been read yet.
